About Wordwave

Why I built a typing game that fights back

Wordwave began with one question: what if practising the keyboard felt less like a drill and more like holding a line against an invasion? This page is the short story behind the game — the idea, the theme, and what is actually inside it.

01 The short version

  • Wordwave is a free typing defense arcade game that runs in your browser.
  • Words fall like enemies; you type them to shoot them down before they cross the defense line.
  • It has 11 ships, 9 weapons with real effects, custom word lists and saved stats.
  • No account, no install, no tracking — just a page and a keyboard.
  • Built by one person with plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript.

02 Why I created it

Most typing tools are timers and paragraphs. They measure you, but they never put you in a situation. I wanted the keyboard to feel like a control panel — where a correct letter is a shot and a completed word is an explosion.

I also wanted a game that opens instantly. A school laptop, a phone on a bus, a borrowed computer in a lab — no download, no launcher, no login screen. If the page loads, you can play.

The custom word list came from the same place. I wanted a way to turn a vocabulary list, or a pile of programming terms, into something you play rather than reread. Typing useeffect while it falls toward your ship is a very different kind of memory than reading it in a table.

And honestly, I wanted to finish something real. A complete, polished game on a canvas — with sound, settings, saved progress and no framework standing between the idea and the screen.

03 The theme

The name says it plainly: words arrive in waves, and you hold the line with a keyboard. Everything else is built to make that one idea feel good.

The palette is deep-space navy, with cyan-teal for your ship and the letters you have already typed, amber for targeting and danger, and plain white for what is still left to type. That single colour rule means you can read the state of a word — and your own accuracy — at a glance.

  • Deep space · #050a11 — the void behind everything
  • Teal · #58f0cf — your ship, typed letters, lock-on
  • Amber · #ffb44a — targeting rings, wave banners, warnings
  • Mist · #9db6bb — supporting text and HUD labels

The HUD is styled like an arcade cockpit: score, wave, hull, accuracy and streak, with a dashed defense line at the bottom that you never want a word to reach. When a word gets through, the screen flashes red — a small, physical reminder that the line moved.

The ship names are a quiet nod to Indian aerospace and mythology. Garuda is the eagle, Pushpaka the flying palace, Ratha the chariot, Tejas radiance, Vajra the thunderbolt, Vyoma the sky. They sit alongside the classic Interceptor, Scout, Guardian and Comet — a fleet that feels like it came from somewhere, not nowhere.

05 How it was built

Wordwave is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript on a single <canvas>. There is no framework, no bundler and no build step. The canvas rescales to fit the window, stays crisp on high-density screens, and the whole game loop runs on requestAnimationFrame.

  • Rendering: Canvas 2D for the playfield, DOM for menus and HUD.
  • Storage: localStorage for settings, stats and loadout — nothing leaves your device.
  • Audio: HTML audio elements for music and effects, Web Audio for small synthesised cues.
  • Ads: a single Google AdSense placement, so the game can stay free.

In short: a small, honest game built to be read, played and understood — not a black box.

06 Play in thirty seconds

  1. Press New game on the title screen.
  2. Type the first letter of any falling word to lock on.
  3. Keep typing — every correct letter fires a shot.
  4. Finish the word to destroy it. Don't let three reach the line.

The full rules, weapon list and tips live on the How to play section of this page — or just start a run and learn by doing.
